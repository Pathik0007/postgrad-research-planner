How this works. Postgraduate Research Planner keeps everything in this browser — nothing you type is uploaded anywhere, so fill in your profile once and every other section reads from it automatically. An optional AI draft button (marked with a spark) asks Claude directly where a step benefits from a second pair of eyes, and always leaves the result editable before you use it anywhere. For finding real people — supervisors, faculty, labs — the tool never guesses names or emails; it builds targeted search links to Google Scholar, ResearchGate, ORCID and university sites so you find and verify the actual person yourself, and every outbound link opens the real site in a new tab. Researcher Arena, at the top of the sidebar, adds the evidence layer — scholarly-graph and dataset search, research gaps, citations, compute guidance and more — held to the same rule.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a PhD supervisor?

Pick your research interests in your profile, then open Find Supervisors. It builds live searches across Google Scholar, ORCID, LinkedIn, ResearchGate and site-restricted university faculty pages so you find and vet a real person yourself — check their last two years of publications and current funding before reaching out.

How do I write a PhD research proposal?

Use the Proposal Builder's nine guided sections — title, background, research questions, significance, methodology, research environment, timeline, ethics and references. Each section explains what it needs, and most can draft a starting point from your profile that you edit before using.

How do I email a PhD supervisor?

Open Outreach Emails, choose "cold introduction," and fill in what you know about the recipient. Keep the first email under 200 words with one clear ask, and wait two to three weeks before a single polite follow-up.

What's the difference between an academic CV and a resume?

An academic CV is comprehensive and grows over your career — publications, talks, grants, teaching — with no length limit. A resume is one to two pages, cut down to what's relevant to a specific role. See CV & Résumé for both, with real template links.

How much GPU memory (VRAM) do I need to train or run a model?

Roughly: parameters × bytes-per-parameter for your precision (4 bytes for FP32, 2 for FP16/BF16, 1 for INT8), ×1.2 for activations and overhead if you're only running inference, or ×~4.8 if you're training (optimizer states and gradients add up fast). Researcher Arena's compute tool does this calculation for your exact model size and precision.

How do I know if my dataset's classes are imbalanced?

Compare the largest class to the smallest in your target column: an imbalance ratio much above 3:1 usually needs handling (resampling, class weights, or a metric other than plain accuracy). Paste a CSV into Researcher Arena's dataset corner for a real, in-browser count of rows, missing values, duplicates and class balance — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

What is the Researcher Arena?

A set of research-planning-and-development tools above the core workflow: scholarly-graph and people search, a dataset corner with a real in-browser dataset health check, an evidence-grounded research-gap finder, a multi-style citation generator, compute/GPU guidance with no invented prices, a reproducibility checklist, a statistical-test advisor, and a literature-matrix builder. Open it from the top of the sidebar.

Does this tool store or share my information?

No. Everything is saved only in this browser's local storage. Nothing is uploaded to a server, and the tool has no login or tracking.

How to evaluate a potential supervisor

Before you write to anyone, check whether they’ve published in the last 18–24 months (an inactive profile is a real signal), whether they currently hold funding or grants that could support a student, roughly how many students they’re already supervising, and whether their recent papers actually match what you want to work on rather than just their general department. A quick scan of their last three papers' introductions usually tells you more than their bio page does.

Search technique: every faculty-page link above appends site: to a normal Google search — e.g. graph neural networks faculty site:edu.au. Swap the region to change the domain, or edit the query directly in your browser's address bar.

Etiquette that actually matters

Keep the first email under 200 words, make one clear ask, and attach your CV rather than describing it. Give a supervisor two to three weeks before a single polite follow-up — not sooner. Personalise every email around something specific in their recent work; a generic email to twenty names is easy to spot and easy to ignore.

Further reading: FindAPhD — contacting supervisors · a community cold-email guideline (GitHub)

Portfolio

Build a research portfolio website

A portfolio earns its place when a reader can click through to something real — a paper, a repo, a demo — in under two clicks. Pick a format that matches how much you want to maintain it.

Choose a format

What to include, roughly in this order

A one-line summary of who you are and what you work on; two to four featured projects or papers with a link and a sentence on your specific contribution; your publications with working links to Scholar/ORCID/DOI; a compact skills list; brief experience/education; and a contact email. Keep the visible language natural — let the work imply your direction rather than stating it outright.
